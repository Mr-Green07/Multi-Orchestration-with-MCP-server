# Abstract

Grounding a generated sentence in a retrieved passage is necessary but not
sufficient: the passage may mention the entity without supporting the claim. We
introduce a post-hoc validator that re-reads each cited passage and rejects any
citation whose quotation cannot be located in it. On a 500-answer sample, the
validator removes 71 percent of unsupported citations while removing only 4 percent
of supported ones.

# 1 Introduction

Hallucination in retrieval-augmented systems is usually discussed as a generation
problem, but a large share of unsupported claims originate in the citation step: the
model attaches a real passage to a claim the passage does not make. Studies of
faithfulness that only check whether a citation points to a real document therefore
overestimate reliability.

# 2 Related Work

Attribution evaluation distinguishes between citation existence, citation relevance
and claim support. Existence checks are cheap and nearly saturated in modern
systems. Support checks require comparing the claim against the quoted span, which
we do with token overlap and a verbatim substring test.

# 3 Methods

The validator receives a claim and a quotation with its source chunk. It normalises
whitespace, tests whether the quotation appears verbatim in the chunk, and otherwise
computes the fraction of quotation terms present in the chunk. A quotation is accepted
when it appears verbatim or when term coverage reaches 0.9. Accepted citations are
then scored for claim support by comparing claim terms against quotation terms, with
a threshold of 0.45. We evaluate on 500 answers sampled from a scientific question
answering benchmark, annotated by two annotators with agreement measured by Cohen's
kappa of 0.81.

# 4 Results

The validator removes 71 percent of unsupported citations and 4 percent of supported
ones. The resulting precision of the surviving citation set is 0.94, compared with
0.68 before validation. The verbatim test alone catches 58 percent of unsupported
citations, and the coverage threshold catches the remaining 13 percent. Validator
latency is 6 ms per answer on CPU, which is negligible relative to generation.

# 5 Discussion

Validation is cheap because it is a string comparison, not a second generation.
Its limitation is that it can only catch citations that are unsupported by the quoted
span; it cannot detect a claim that is unsupported by the paper but quoted correctly
from a different context.

# 6 Limitations

Our annotators saw only the quotation and the claim, not the full paper, so they may
have marked some claims unsupported that the paper supports elsewhere. The threshold
values were tuned on the same sample we report, which risks optimistic bias, and we
did not evaluate on answers generated by models other than the one used to sample the
benchmark.

# 7 Conclusion

Post-hoc citation validation is a cheap, effective filter, and it should be a default
component of any grounded answering system that claims to cite its sources.
